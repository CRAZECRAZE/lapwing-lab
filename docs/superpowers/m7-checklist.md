# M7 checks on your real data

Only you can do these, because they compare the app with how a run feels at speed, with your own texts and with what you really practised today, in a real browser and on the real Uni. What was checked here: the calculations (ghost times, picking the best and last run, the text matching and the part cutting, the routine ticks and the routine checks), the routes and the three server files, by tests that use temp folders and made-up strokes only, never your `data/`. What could not be checked here: how the ghost feels at speed and whether you want it there at all, whether the coverage figure on a real article matches your sense of how much of it you can stroke, whether the routine ticks match what you did, and how any of it behaves with your real dictionary of habits and your real saved texts. Start the app with `npm start` and use a Chromium-based browser.

Each check says whether it needs the board. A run needs the board, because a run only moves when strokes arrive from the Uni; looking at pages, pasting, saving, deleting and planning a routine do not. Where a check is marked **board**, you press real strokes; where it is marked **no board**, you only click and type in the browser.

Words used below: a **run** is one test in History. A **part** is one block of up to 100 words of a text. A **ghost** is the pace of an earlier run of the same drill (see the README, "Ghosts"). **Done** on a routine means a finished run, not one left early, with that step's mode and section, started on today's local date.

Nothing in this milestone writes anything except these: a run (a row in `tests` and its rows in `strokes`, in `data/steno.db`), as for any drill; a saved text as `data/texts/<id>.json`; your routines as `data/routines.json`; and the `ghost` field of `data/settings.json` when you press a Ghost choice. Pasting, checking and starting a text you do not save writes nothing. A ghost, a coverage figure and a tick are worked out each time from your archive, your dictionary and the text, and are never stored.

## Ghosts

1. **A ghost on the second run, not on the first (board).** Pick a free-type amount you have never run on the source you will use (the **Custom** box, for example 12 words, is the easy way to get one that is new). Finish one run. Its screen shows no `Ghost` line and no two thin bars. Start **Again** or the same amount afresh and finish a second run: from the first stroke a line such as `Ghost (best, today) 3 · You 2` shows under the status line, with two thin bars under it. A first run of fewer than 5 completed words does not count as something to race, and a run left early (Esc) never does, so if no ghost appears, check that the first run was finished and had at least 5 words. A different words source or a different amount has its own ghost.
2. **Other drills (board, you may skip some).** Do two runs of a section drill (`/drill/list/<list>`), a chapter test and a Stage 0 drill, each twice. The second run of each shows a ghost line. If one of them never does, tell me which.
3. **The Ghost picker, and that it is remembered (no board for the picker, board for what it does).** On a free-type, drill or text screen the pickers row has **Ghost: best · last · off** beside the board and Hint pickers, with `best` pressed by default. Press **off** and start a run: no ghost line. Press **last** and start: the line says `Ghost (last, ...)`. Reload the page and open a drill again: the Ghost picker shows the choice you left, and the run follows it. The choice is stored as `ghost` in `data/settings.json`. **Best** is the run with the highest wpm among the 30 most recent runs of that drill that have at least 5 words; **last** is the most recent of them. If you have a run that is clearly faster than your latest one and **best** and **last** race the same ghost, tell me.
4. **The ghost freezes with the drill (board).** Start a run with a ghost and press a few strokes so the ghost count has started to climb. Open a dock tool (`Alt+/` for Lookup): the drill says it is paused and the ghost count stops, and when you close the tool it carries on from where it was. Do the same by unplugging the board (the banner appears and the clock stops within about a second; the ghost stops with it) and by plugging it back in. The ghost and the clock must not run on while the drill is paused.
5. **No ghost in a fix-up run (board).** Open **Fix-up** from the Practice tab and start a run. There is no Ghost picker, no ghost line and no bars, and the result card has no ghost line. Do this once whatever your ghost setting is.
6. **The ghost line and the result card line (board).** Finish a run that raced a ghost. While it ran, the line read `Ghost (best, 3 days ago) 14 · You 12` (the ghost's name, when that run was, the words the ghost had by now, and yours). On the result card one neutral line follows: `Best run (3 days ago) had 22 words by 0:41. You: 25 — 3 ahead.` (or `behind`, or `level`). Check that the numbers agree with what you saw: the time is how long your run took, the ghost's count is how many words that earlier run had by that time, and yours is the number of words you completed. A run with no ghost has no line. Tell me if a number looks wrong or the wording reads like advice.
7. **How it feels at speed (board, a judgement only you can make).** Race a ghost for a few runs. Does the line and the two bars help or distract at your typing distance? Is it too quiet to notice or too present? A ghost's times come from the clock times of the strokes of the old run, so a break you took in that run (you stepped away for a minute) is inside its times and the ghost looks slow. If a ghost you are racing seems to be far slower than the run you remember, that is the cause. Tell me what you want changed.

## Real text

8. **Paste a text with numbers and an unknown word and read the coverage (no board).** Open **Practice**, then **Open real text** on the Real text card (or open `/practice/text`). Paste a paragraph of your own that has a number (`42`), a symbol on its own (`&`), an invented word (`zzorbl`, twice) and a hyphenated word. Press **Check**. You see `N of M words can be stroked (P%)`, then `Numbers and symbols skipped: 2`, then `Words with no entry in the dictionary:` with `zzorbl ×2` first (most frequent first, then alphabetical). The numbers and the `&` are not counted as words. A word you typed with a capital or a trailing comma should still be found. If more than 20 words are missing only the first 20 are listed and a line says `and N more`. A percentage is never 100 while a word is missing and never 0 while one can be stroked. Press **Check** with nothing pasted: it says there is no text to check yet.
9. **Does the coverage match your sense of the text (no board, a judgement only you can make).** Paste a real article or email you read. Read the figure and the missing words. Does the share sound right to you? Are the missing words ones the dictionary should have (names, plurals, forms with an apostrophe) or genuinely rare? Remember that the match is against the official Lapwing dictionary only, never your own Plover dictionaries, and that a missing word is skipped in the drill, not fingerspelled. Tell me about any word that is listed as missing and that Lookup finds when you type it.
10. **A 230-word text has 3 parts (no board).** Paste a text of exactly 230 words that are all in the dictionary (for example `the cat ` pasted 115 times), press **Check** (`230 of 230 words can be stroked (100%)`), give it a name such as `Test230` and press **Save**. In **Saved texts** click its name: it opens part 1, which says `Part 1 of 3` and `100 words in this part`. Open `/practice/text/run/test230/3` in the address bar: it says `Part 3 of 3` and `30 words in this part`. Open `/practice/text/run/test230/4`: it says `This text has no part 4.` and **Back to text** is there. A text whose words are partly missing has fewer drill words, so fewer parts.
11. **Run a part (board).** Start part 1 of a text of your own. The words come in the order of the text, one flowing line, not shuffled, and a word you cannot stroke is not there. On finishing, the result card shows Accuracy, Speed, Words and Time, the ghost line (once you have run the same part before), **Add a note**, **Next part** (when there is one), **Again** and **Back to text**. **Next part** opens part 2.
12. **A saved text survives a reload, an unsaved one does not (no board).** Paste a text, press **Check** and then **Start** without saving: the address is `/practice/text/run/pasted/1`. Reload the page: it says `There is no pasted text here.` (an unsaved text lives in memory only). Now save a text, reload `/practice/text`, and it is still in **Saved texts**; click it and it opens.
13. **Deleting asks first (no board).** In **Saved texts** press **Delete** on a text. A dialog asks `Delete this text?` and names it. **Cancel**, Esc or a click outside it keeps the text. Press **Delete** again and confirm: the text goes from the list, and runs of it in History are still there (their label then shows the text's id in place of its name).
14. **A text named "Pasted" (no board).** Paste any text, name it `Pasted` and save it. It appears in **Saved texts** as `Pasted`, and opening it from there loads the saved text, not a leftover unsaved one (it is saved with the id `pasted-2`, because `pasted` is the id of the unsaved text; if you look at `data/texts/` you will see `pasted-2.json`). A second text with the same name as an earlier one gets `-2`, `-3` in its id in the same way.
15. **A text run in History (board).** Finish a run of a saved text, then open **History**. The run reads `Text · <name> · part N` with its accuracy and speed; a run of an unsaved text reads `Text · pasted · part N`. The same label is in the Days list for that day and on the run's own page. A run of a text counts in the day's time and strokes, and under Progress with **Mode** `Everything` for speed, but not under `Free type` or `Drills and tests`.
16. **The limits (no board, you may skip this).** A text over 200,000 characters is refused at **Check** with a message saying so. A 51st saved text, or a name over 60 characters or empty, cannot be saved (Save is off and a line says why). These are tested; I would not spend time on them by hand.

## Routines

17. **Make a routine with all four kinds of step (no board).** Open **Practice** and **Open routine** or **Plan a routine** on the Routine card (or open `/practice/routine`). Press **New routine**, give it a name, and add a step of each kind from **Add a step**: a **Section drill** (a list from a chapter you have reached), **Free type** (a source and a mode from the four presets), **Fix-up** (words or patterns) and **Real text** (a saved text and a part). Use **Move up**, **Move down** and **Remove** on them. Press **Save**. The routine page shows it as a checklist with `0 of 4 done today`, the first step marked `Next up`, the others `To do`, and a **Start** link on every one. The first routine you save becomes the active one on its own; a later one is made active with **Make active**. **Cancel** in the editor drops what you changed.
18. **The ticks as runs finish (board).** On the routine page press **Start** on the first step, finish the run, and use **Back to routine**. The step is `Done` with a mark, the count reads `1 of 4 done today` and `Next up` moves on. Do the other steps the same way (the fix-up step opens the fix-up page: start its list from there and finish the run). A step done by the routine's own Start link and the same drill done some other way (from the Book or Practice tab) both tick. A run left early (Esc) does not tick. Compare the ticks with what you really did: tell me about a step that ticked and should not have, or the other way round. If two steps point at the same list, amount or text part, they tick together.
19. **The ticks reset the next day (no board, wait or move the date).** Look at the routine page the day after: every step is `To do`. Nothing stored the ticks, so a routine done yesterday starts again. To see it sooner, change the computer's date to tomorrow and reload the page; set the date back afterwards. Do not run a drill while the date is wrong, because a run is stored with the date of the moment it was run. If you would rather not move the date, skip this check.
20. **Deleting a text used by a routine breaks nothing (no board).** Put a Real text step in a routine, then delete that saved text on `/practice/text`. Open the routine page: the step reads `Unavailable`, has no **Start** link, is not `Next up` and is not counted as done, and the page and the Practice card still load. In the editor the step carries an `Unavailable` note and can be removed. A step whose section list or free-type source no longer exists is shown the same way.
21. **Back to routine (board for the result card).** From the routine page press **Start** on a section drill, a free-type step and a text step. On its result card each page says **Back to routine** where it would otherwise say Back to text, Back to Practice or Back to Chapter N, and the link goes to `/practice/routine`. Esc out of the drill goes there too. On a text part's result card, **Next part** keeps you in the routine (its **Back to routine** is still there). A fix-up step opens the fix-up page and does not carry it. The same pages opened some other way say their usual link.
22. **The Practice cards (no board).** On **Practice**, after the Fix-up and Free type cards, a **Real text** card (`Practise words from a text you paste or save.`, link **Open real text**) and a **Routine** card follow. The Routine card shows the active routine's name and `N of M done today` and **Open routine**; with no routine it says `No routine yet.` and **Plan a routine**; with routines but none active it says `No routine is active.`
23. **The planner (no board, you may skip parts).** On the routine page: **Delete** on a routine asks `Delete routine?` first and deletes only after you confirm; deleting the active routine leaves no routine active (`No routine is active.`) until you press **Make active** on another. A ninth routine is refused with a note (`You can keep up to 8 routines.`), and a thirteenth step with `A routine can have up to 12 steps.` An empty name keeps **Save** off. With the server stopped, **Save** says it could not save and to check the server, with nothing alarming in it.

## Files

24. **Where things are stored (no board).** After the checks above, look in `data/`: saved texts are `data/texts/<id>.json` (`{name, text}`, one file each), your routines are `data/routines.json` (`{routines: [{id, name, steps}], active}`), and the Ghost choice is the `ghost` field in `data/settings.json`. The folder `data/texts/` appears when you first save a text and `data/routines.json` when you first save a routine. Opening the Real text or Routine page, pressing **Check** and starting an unsaved text create nothing. Apart from these, nothing in M7 writes anything but the ordinary run rows. Back up by copying `data/` as before; the files are plain JSON, so you can read them in any editor, but a text file edited by hand that no longer has a string `name` and a string `text` is treated as not there, and a `routines.json` that does not pass the same checks is treated as no routines.

Tell me about anything that is hard to read, any ghost that seems to be the wrong run, any coverage figure that disagrees with what you expect, any tick that disagrees with what you did, and anything you would want a routine to do that it does not.
