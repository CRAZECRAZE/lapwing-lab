# M6b checks on your real history

Only you can do these, because they compare the app with what you remember and with your real history, in a real browser. What was checked here: the calculations (by the tests, which use temp folders and made-up days only, never your `data/`), and that the code reads from the archive and writes only what is listed under check 15. What could not be checked here: whether the rhythm, the month's numbers and the chapter dates are right for your own runs, how the pages look and read at your distance and in your light, and the plateau notice on real history (you have practiced since 26 October, so it cannot appear yet). Start the app with `npm start` and use a Chromium-based browser. Nothing in this milestone writes to `data/` except your report notes and the two settings fields listed in check 15, so you can open and switch things as much as you like.

Words used below: a **run** is one test in History (a free-type run, a drill or a chapter test). **Finished** means it was not left early and was not cut off by closing the tab. A **plateau** is a stretch of weeks in which your free-type speed stayed about the same.

## The rhythm fixes

1. **The `start` bar.** Open **History**, open any finished run, and look at the Rhythm strip. The first bar is labelled `start` (hover or Tab to it: `Stroke 1`, the pause, `start`) and the note under the strip says `Started after N s`. If you sat for a few seconds before the first key, the bar is tall but not red, and the run's `Stalls` figure does not count it. Under `Show the numbers` the first row's kind is `start`, never `stall` or `break`.
2. **A chapter test says `repeats only`.** Open a finished chapter test in History. Under `Steadiness` the figure carries the tag `repeats only`, and it no longer reads erratic: it is nearer what you score in free type than the 18 or so M6a showed. Open a drill or a free-type run: no tag.
3. **First showings are labelled.** In the same chapter test, the first time each word appears (about one in three: the very first stroke is the `start` bar, not a first showing) is drawn as a paler dashed bar. If you stepped away for more than 10 seconds before a word's first showing, that pause is drawn as a dotted `break` line instead, and counts in the run's `breaks`. Hover or Tab to one: the note says `first showing`. The note under the strip says how many pale dashed bars there are and that they are left out of the scores. In a free-type run or a drill there are none.
4. **History and Progress agree.** On Progress choose `Drills and tests` and pick that same chapter test in the Rhythm panel's `Run` list. The steadiness, stalls and typical gap match what History shows for it (same figures, same `repeats only` tag). Under `Accuracy and consistency`, in `Drills and tests` and `Everything`, one line says `Chapter tests count their repeats only.`

## Where the reports are

5. **The link.** On Progress, next to the Range and Mode switches, there is a `Reports` link. Click it: the address becomes `/progress/reports` and **Progress** is still the highlighted tab in the top bar (the top bar still has six tabs).
6. **The list.** `/progress/reports` lists the months newest first, from your earliest month with practice or a note to the current one. A month with neither (a gap) is left out, and a month with only a note is listed (a note of only spaces does not count). Each row shows the month (`October 2026`), the practice time, the strokes (`N strokes`), and the word `note` if that month has a note. Only October exists so far, so there is one row. Compare its time and strokes with Days for October. Click the row. Then type a month that does not exist into the address bar, such as `/progress/reports/2026-13`: the page says `No such month.` with a `Back to Reports` link, and **Progress** is still the highlighted tab.

## The October report

7. **The header and the six tiles.** The page says `October 2026 · N practice days`, where N is the number of days in October on which you practiced (count the shaded days on Days). Under it are six tiles in this order: `Practice time`, `Strokes`, `Free-type wpm`, `Accuracy`, `Steadiness`, `Chapters passed`.
8. **The numbers behind the tiles.** `Practice time` and `Strokes` cover every mode and match Days (time to within about a minute per day, as in M6a check 5). `Free-type wpm` and `Accuracy` are the averages of October's finished free-type runs, weighted by the words in each; accuracy has one decimal as on Progress. `Steadiness` is the middle value of the days that have one. A tile that rests on fewer than three free-type runs shows a dash and `not enough runs yet`; it never shows `NaN` or a made-up zero.
9. **A first month compares with nothing.** Because October is your first month, no tile says `vs September`. The only lines under tiles are `first day X, last day Y` under `Free-type wpm` (whole wpm) and under `Accuracy` (with one decimal), shown once at least two days have a free-type run. X is the first free-type day's speed and Y the last day's. `Practice time`, `Strokes` and `Steadiness` have no line. In November, the November report compares with October (`+... vs October`, in the plain colour, never red).
10. **Chapters passed.** `Chapters passed` counts the chapters you first passed in October, and the line under it names them: Chapter 4's pass appears as `ch 4 on the 30th` (or the day you actually passed it). The date is the first time you met the pass mark (or, if you marked it read or overrode it, the date of the mark), in your local time. A chapter the Ladder shows as cleared only because a later result was good enough, with no run that passed and no mark, has no date and is not counted here. If a number is wrong, tell me which chapter and when you passed it.
11. **The chart and the scopes line.** Under the tiles is the `Wpm by day` chart for October only (the axis runs from 1 to 31 October). Its `Show the numbers` table has the same days and values as the dots. Below it a line says `Time and strokes cover every mode; speed, accuracy and steadiness cover free type.`

## The notes

12. **Typing a note.** Under the chart there is a box labelled `Your notes`. Type a sentence or two. A moment after you stop it says `Saved`. Reload the page: your text is still there. Then open `data/reports/2026-10.md` in a text editor (it is a plain UTF-8 file with exactly what you typed, nothing else; the folder `data/reports/` was created by your first save). Change the file by hand, reload the report, and your edit shows in the box. Back on `/progress/reports` the October row now carries `note`.

## The month-end banner

13. **The banner.** On 30 October, Progress shows no banner: October is not over. On 31 October (or any later day, even after a month with no practice, while October is the newest month with practice that you have not opened), Progress shows a quiet line under the switches: `October is done. Your report is ready.`, linking to the report. Click it, and the report opens. Go back to Progress: the line is gone and stays gone on later visits (it is `reportSeen` in `data/settings.json`). It never shows for a month before that month's last day. Opening the report of the current month before its last day, or opening an older month, does not hide a later banner and does not make an earlier one reappear. If you never open October's report the line stays until you open it.

## The plateau notice

14. **No notice yet, and when it can come.** Progress shows no plateau notice above the tiles, and neither the Book's chapter pages nor `Where you are` on the Practice tab show the gold `Speed flat for N weeks, see Progress` line in the chapter panel. This is correct: the notice looks only at free type over the last 56 days, in eight 7-day weeks ending today, and it needs at least 2 free-type runs in each of 3 of those weeks with the weeks' speeds within 2 wpm (or 4% of this week's speed, whichever is more) of this week's. A week with fewer than 2 free-type runs is skipped, not counted against you, so a week off does not hide it. So it cannot appear before about three weeks of regular free-type practice, and there is no switch to show it sooner. It needs 2 or more free-type runs in each of 3 weeks, flat within 2 wpm or 4%. When it does appear: the title is `Your speed has been flat for N weeks`; the body gives free-type wpm for this week and up to two earlier weeks; `Plateaus like this are common while learning steno.`; a `Meanwhile ...` line only if your steadiness rose by 3 points or your accuracy by 1.5 points over those weeks (never a line about something getting worse); the links `Weakest keys` and `Mix-ups` scroll to those panels; and it gives no advice. The Range and Mode switches do not change or hide it. Click `Not now (14 days)`: it disappears from Progress, from the Book's chapter pages and from `Where you are` on the Practice tab (reload each to check), and stays away for 14 days. The snooze is shared by all of them. It also disappears by itself when your speed moves out of the flat stretch.

## What is written to `data/`

15. **Only these.** M6b adds three things to `data/`: the plain files `data/reports/YYYY-MM.md` (one per month you write a note in), and two fields in `data/settings.json`: `plateauSnoozedUntil` (a date, set by `Not now (14 days)`) and `reportSeen` (a month like `2026-10`, set when you open a finished month's report). Check them: before you type a note there is no `data/reports/` folder, and before you open a finished month's report `reportSeen` is absent or null in `data/settings.json` (saving any setting rewrites the whole file with the new fields as null). Mind the order: on 31 October, opening the report for checks 10 and 12 already stores `reportSeen`, so look at the file first if you want to see it absent. Opening Progress, the reports list or a report does not create any file; the only write on opening a report is `reportSeen`, and only for a month that is over. Nothing in `steno.db` changes: the numbers in a report are recomputed from the archive each time, and only your notes are stored.
